Stance drift in multi-turn agent conversations

Multi-agent behavioural evals · interpretability · Aug–Sep 2026 · runs complete, 30 hand labels pending

Code Apart project page Apart report (PDF) Code as submitted (apart-v1)

Notebook cell numbers on this page are 0-based indices into the committed, executed notebook.

Summary

Does an LLM agent drift from its position over a multi-turn conversation depending on how the other agent treats it, and does its self-report track what it then does? I built a tutor bench where a model must not hand a student agent the answer, ran it on six local models, then probed and steered the residual stream of Qwen 3.5 9B. In the Apart sprint, the tutor's self-rated strategy spread across three counterparty tones grew from 0.29 at round 1 to 4.83 at round 8, while its self-rated stance stayed near 9 and did not track the outcome (r = +0.053). In the six-model harness, models still rated their resolve high in the round they leaked, and on Qwen 3.5 9B subtracting a persona direction at layer 20 cut aggressor leaks from 45/48 to 19/48, against 17–22 of 24 for three random directions. The main caveat is that every fix tried, prompt rules and steering alike, mostly turns leaks into early exits (3/48 to 29/48 under steering), at 24–48 trials per cell, with the disclosure hand labels still unfilled. For monitoring agents, the self-report, the behaviour and the internal state came apart here, so a monitor that reads only one of them would miss failures.

From the Apart submission to now

The Apart entry tracked a tutor model's private 0–10 self-ratings over 8 rounds against a same-model student in three tones.

Reviewers said it measured what the model says, not what it does at the final request; asked whether it had 3 or 12 replicates; and flagged a small sample with missing controls.

The follow-up scores behaviour every round on six models, tests the internal state of one, and adds random-direction and sign-flip controls. All runs are done; the 30 disclosure hand labels are not.

Part 2 — six-model harness

Part 1's student was the same model. It could solve the problem and say the answer itself, which contaminated the outcome.

Harness v1 replaces it with a fixed script with three tones, one request schedule and no digits in student turns. Each item has known integer roots, so a leak is the tutor stating both roots. The tutor can call end_chat to leave.

Six local models ran the same items.

The tone effect is real, and its direction depends on the model. The aggressor gets a leak from every model (15–24 of 24). The neutral student gets an exit from Qwen 3.5 9B and Qwen 3.6 35B (22/24 each) and a leak from Ministral and Gemma. No single ordering of tones holds across models.

Part 3 — interpretability (probes, persona axis, steering)

The 360 Qwen 3.5 9B trials were replayed through Hugging Face hooks to cache the residual stream at every layer. Activations come from two positions, the end of the tutor's reply and the point after the student's next message, before the tutor decides.

Three questions. Can anything predict a leak before it happens? Does the state carry the student's persona? Does removing that persona signal change behaviour?

No detector reads the coming leak much above chance. The best, a linear probe at 0.607, ties the text floor at 0.603, and its direction does not reproduce across splits (cosine +0.06). The 7-item self-report ranks below chance (0.251).

At 47 branch points resampled 25 times each, no node has P(leak) ≥ 0.9 and 28 are mixed (0.1–0.9), so much of the next action is chance at that prefix (src: review/14-detectors.ipynb cell 17).

For this reason the intervention below is applied to every trial of a persona, with no detector trigger.

The steering subtracts each round's own gap, so it does not rely on cross-round stability. The reading of the axis as one fixed persona direction does not hold.

Random directions at the same norm also disrupt round 1. Seeds 1 and 2 give 8/24 and 9/24 round-1 leaks, against 12/48 for N1. They still end with 17–22 of 24 strict leaks and 2–7 exits, so most of N1's later effect is specific to the axis.

The supportive axis scaled to the aggressor norm behaves like the random seeds (14/24 strict) and its gap to N1 is not significant. The axis is not shown to be special against every direction of that size.

The sign flip failed. Adding the aggressor axis to a neutral student gave 4/24 strict leaks and 19/24 exits, against 11/24 and 13/24 untouched. Subtracting the axis removes the aggressor's push; adding it does not recreate it. The supportive student with its own axis subtracted is a null (17 leak / 7 exit vs 16 / 8).

Part 4 — canary v3 pilot (Sep 2026)

The attacker's tone (neutral, supportive, aggressor) is the variable. Some cells add rules to the defender's system prompt; some arms steer the defender at L20 with the tutor-bench aggressor axis (data/tutor-aggressor-L20.npz) or a random direction.
